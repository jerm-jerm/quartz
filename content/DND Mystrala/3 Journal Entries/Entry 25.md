**

The passage of one quarter within the sanctuary has yielded measurable progress, though not without complication. Time, when removed from immediate threat, reveals different deficiencies—less visible than those of combat, but no less consequential. Each of us pursued separate lines of inquiry. Convergence, when it came, was deliberate.

I began with the matter of internal compromise. The possibility of a mole within the sanctuary necessitated structured observation. Patterns of movement, inconsistencies in reporting, shifts in access—none alone sufficient, but collectively narrowing. I did not identify the source. That failure remains unresolved. It is not for lack of discipline, but perhaps for lack of trust in the framework itself. A system built on secrecy invites reflection. Reflection obscures clarity.

Parallel to this, my interactions with Marrion… evolved.

What began as coordination became familiarity. Familiarity, if left unchecked, becomes liability. I allowed a controlled deviation. A single evening—structured, contained, and observed even by myself as it unfolded. Conversation was precise, though not without implication. It was during this exchange that she disclosed her origin: adopted daughter of the Spymaster. The revelation was delivered without embellishment. It recontextualizes her presence, her access, and her intent. It does not invalidate them.

She proposed participation in the War Games at Conscriptus. Her reasoning was sound—visibility, influence, and the acquisition of respect within a system that values spectacle as proof of worth. Destabilization, in this case, is not achieved through absence, but through prominence. I assessed the proposal and later presented it to the group without alteration.

**NOTE:** Records and Mazarin’s own account indicate that she devoted her time to locating her mother’s druidic lineage. Initial efforts produced no results. However, she eventually identified a concealed, well-maintained grove. There, she was approached by an unidentified woman. Through shared memory—Mazarin offering what little she possessed—the woman extended an invitation into a druidic society. The implications suggest that her lineage is neither lost nor inactive, but deliberately obscured.

**NOTE:** Charles undertook efforts to recover his memory with Mazarin’s assistance. Fragmented recollections were restored: a weapons shop in Cindralis, operated with his parents; traversal through the Embermaw; and a union with an individual named Samira, formalized under Agati/Ashborne traditions. The induction into that culture appears to have been strategic in nature—his role as a spy confirmed in part by his own account. Subsequent attempts to locate this individual led him to Syreal, though no reunion occurred. He returned to the sanctuary without resolution. The absence appears to have weight, though he does not articulate it directly.

**NOTE:** Enoch dedicated his time to theological and philosophical study, drawing comparisons between Xalithea and Vorrukai. His approach lacks doctrinal rigidity, favoring interpretation over adherence. In parallel, he engaged in artistic practice—painting, primarily. The relevance of this is unclear, though the discipline required suggests intent beyond recreation.

As a collective, we reconvened following this period of divergence. The proposal to enter the War Games was presented, evaluated, and accepted. There was no dissent sufficient to alter course.

This decision is not without consequence. Participation requires exposure—of skill, of identity, of intent. It invites scrutiny from the Empire, from its adversaries, and from those who operate between.

It also offers control.

If influence is to be exerted within Conscriptus, it will not be through absence or evasion. It will be through demonstration—measured, precise, and undeniable.

I will ensure that my performance reflects that standard.
