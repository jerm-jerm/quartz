We departed early the next morning by train back toward Ledgerman Headquarters in Logistica. Marrion, notably, did not see us off. Upon arrival in Blackspire, we proceeded to the Citadel of Auditors, where blocks reconvene between assignments. The majority were engaged in clerical work.

Charles wandered toward a purple-haired, blue-skinned female and her elven companion.  
**NOTE:** Witness accounts suggest the two mocked him in a manner that could be read as sarcastic or dismissive. Charles, however, remained composed, his tone level. Even offering a remark that bordering on flirtation.

At our station, we discovered a note instructing us to meet Horatio in the mess hall. En route, I examined Marrion’s reports. They fulfilled the entirety of the paperwork for mission completion, but the narrative had been significantly reframed in our favor. Admiral Katharis drilled into me that such twisting of narrative was unbecoming of command, for it corrodes trust. Marrion's decision though was deliberate and based on my last conversation with her, I do not believe sentiment is her only motive.

Sal and Mazarin went their own way. Charles and I proceeded together to Horatio. He and I seem to have found a rhythm, perhaps the battlefield equalizes more swiftly than any court or lecture hall.

Horatio briefed us on our next assignment. His former block, 233, disappeared while guarding a package in the Divinorum. The package itself survived untouched, but the ledgermen vanished, their names erased from the ledger, an act requiring significant power. Horatio believes their remains lie within the Embermaw and has charged us with either recovering them or discerning their fate. Due to the general danger of the environment, he has arranged a guide, on loan from The Covenant, an Ashborne named Enoch.

Upon our exit we were confronted by a goliath woman, Nestra, approached with her tiefling companion. She called me “princess,” derided Conscriptus, and boasted of always wanting to kill a Katharis. I asked if this was a formal request for a duel. She did not answer. A human male in her block restrained her and apologized with measured respect. The encounter was an annoyance, though not without utility, it reminded me plainly how my name provokes as much challenge as deference.

We gathered the group and procured supplies. Enoch's status as a dissident became the subject of conversation, but he provided us with clear instructions on preparing for The Embermaw. He speaks with humility, but his posture and phrasing reveal extensive martial training.

Back on The Holy Rail, this time toward Cindralis, I caught a glimpse of Conscriptus. It has been scarcely a week, yet the sight struck me harder than I expected. I allowed myself no sentimentality, I have already sent a letter home, which is more than enough.

Enoch guided us through a mountain pass rich with alien flora and fauna. Sal was characteristically eager, collecting specimens of all types. At one point, Enoch stopped before a monolith he explained was erected at the death of one devoted to the Fire Lord. He knelt to pray.

NOTE: Although Enoch was recorded to speak in a language none of the others are documented to understand. Charles Brand was reportedly able to decipher the meaning of the words. 

On my visualization of the weave around the monolith, the prayer was rejected. Sal, undeterred, insisted on making his own offering. Which Enoch helped him achieve by punching him to draw blood. Flames seared his arm deeply, although he bore the injury with composure and insisted we continue of journey. 

Prior to departure, I cleansed the monolith utilizing a simple spell. It revealed an imaged etched into it. Enoch explained Kosef, the volcano as an embodiment of a god, and its herald, a celestial bursting forth from its depth. A masked concealed the face, wings adorned its back, and twin blades drawn. The likeness to Xalithea, the Forever Blade, was unmistakable. I felt the weight of the resemblance but kept my observation private.

We pressed on, intent on finding a secure spot for camp.
