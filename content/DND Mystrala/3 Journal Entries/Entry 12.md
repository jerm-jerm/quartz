<div style="text-align: left; font-size: 1.25em;"><em>314Fi.2Q.76-78</em></div>

Serraux yet lives, though his general state would have one think otherwise. Beneath the weight of his armor, his body is a map of wounds, skin pale and split. We interrogated and he spoke.  "He would see you, Luminora. Conscriptus has plans for the both of you." Fischer Valantis waits twenty miles north and knows we are coming.

Sal and Enoch tended his wounds, working steadily until, instead of death, he slipped into unconsciousness. We remained in the abandoned shelter through the night as the sandstorm raged outside. 

At dawn, he stirred. His gaze lingered on me for too long, almost reverent. He spoke of the last mission of Block 233, dispatched to the Founder’s vault to dismantle its defenses. He showed me, in a vision, the chrysalis that housed the Founder: a shell of celestial alloy entwined with clockwork. When the constructs guarding it awoke, they tore through his chest.

From that mission they learned too much. The Founder, he said, was no deity enthroned but a prisoner of his own design. The Directorate had hollowed the FETC from within. The Manuals of Ascension for 200Fi, served contradiction rather than clarity. The people have been forsaken.

Valantis and his followers now suggest the binding the Empire beneath Xalithea’s banner instead. Serraux spoke her name as one born in Conscriptus, voice thick with devotion. He pressed into my hand the token that had shown me his vision. 

When Enoch asked of the Agati alliance, Serraux told him that under a new reign, their FETC captors would serve in chains. Enoch, facing away from us, removed his mask and refused that vision.

We left Serraux within the shelter and turned north. The heat was oppressive, and our rations were rapidly depleting. I needed time to prepare a spell for water when Sal, after long bouts of rambling, suddenly remembered the artifact he carried which produced water endlessly. Forgetfulness is a liability that endangers us all. I am begrudgingly grateful.

After a day of traveling, we rested beneath the fossilized bones of an immense dragon, its ribcage arched like a great coliseum entrance. Poppies grew in red waves around it. Sal harvested these Firelash blooms, and I assisted. It felt grounding to work an old skill.

In the morning we pressed on, passing the wreck of a Netherese skyship, _The Empyrean Weave._ Its hull bore claw marks, its arcane batteries missing. Sal scavenged shards of warped glass from the sand.

Eventually, we arrived at Fischer’s location. The remainder of Block 233 awaited within. Fischer greeted us with warmth too easily given. I did not return it. Secession is being spoken aloud in council, but they are still divided. When pressed, he confirmed that House Katharis has yet to declare. I told him I would make no move without my matriarch’s blessing.

Then Vell struck. She severed me from the pruning ritual without warning. Every strand of a connection present for a lifetime, tore away at once, leaving me raw, exposed, unmade. Valantis looked into my face and called it victory. He said my death would be the spark, the death of a noble daughter to ignite rebellion and draw Conscriptus to war.

He does not know though. Pruning was not safety. It showed me where I end, and where I may yet surpass myself. I will show him I am no pawn to be played.
