## Impression
The Oracle’s manner was riddled with evasions. It told us it could not answer why we were chosen, for to do so would alter the path. It promised no reward beyond duty. It hinted at contingencies, pruning threads, replacements should we fail. A contradiction: it claims perfection, yet plans for error.

It veiled its threats against me in terms my lineage. It conceals hostility in the language of prophecy.

When [[Sal Brentwood|Sal]] pressed for his niece, claimed as a dissident, it struck with cruelty. When [[Yedan-Neric|Yedan]] questioned it, its attention sharpened, hungry. The Oracle does not know all things, it searches, pries, feeds upon what is offered. Even its threats reveal limitation.

I consider the Oracle carefully. It may yet be divine, but divinity bound in rust and deception is no surety. Perhaps it is what remains of the Founder’s will, fractured and worn. Perhaps it is something else, wearing his shape. What is plain is this: it is not infallible.

That is useful.

## Known Record
The Oracle is said to divine the future, hence its name. It has long been the instrument by which the The Empire identifies those with a dissidence coefficient too high to tolerate. Such people disappear. What becomes of them has been unknown, but it is now evident they are given to the Oracle itself, fed into whatever machinery sustains it. The legend tells that the Founder shaped it with his mind and will, that its gaze once turned dissidents into monuments of gold, their failures preserved as warnings. To shield us from that brilliance, the Golden Veil was raised, hiding the Oracle in the Vault of Stars. 

Its true form is nothing of the kind. What light and grandeur it projected was a trick of perception. When Charles broke through the glamour, we saw only a rusted sphere, leaking fluid into stagnant water, bound by hydraulic veins. A thing sustained, not sustaining.

It is not its function to select Ledgermen. That it chose us is an aberration.
